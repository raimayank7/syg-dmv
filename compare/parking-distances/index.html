No-parking distances: California, Virginia, Oregon

Parking rules are a list of distances, and the list is slightly different in each state. That makes them easy to test and easy to mix up.

The table shows what the three handbooks say, as we read them on 12 November 2026. "Not in the passages we read" means only that: the handbook may well cover the point elsewhere, so look it up there.

RuleCaliforniaVirginiaOregon
Fire hydrantWithin 15 feetWithin 15 feetWithin 10 feet
Crosswalk or intersectionWithin 20 feet of a marked or unmarked crosswalk (15 feet where there is a curb extension)Within 20 feet of an intersectionWithin 20 feet of a marked or unmarked crosswalk at an intersection
Fire station drivewayWithin 15 feetWithin 15 feet of the entranceWithin 15 feet on the same side, 75 feet on the opposite side
Railroad crossingNot in the passages we readWithin 50 feetNot in the passages we read
Traffic signal or stop signNot in the passages we readNot in the passages we readWithin 50 feet

Colored curbs in California

California's handbook adds a colour code painted on the curb. White: stop only long enough to pick up or drop off passengers. Green: park for a limited time, shown on a sign or on the curb. Yellow: load and unload passengers and freight, for no longer than the time posted. Red: no stopping. The handbook lists a further colour for disabled parking; read that part in the handbook itself.

Parking on a hill

California's handbook explains which way to turn the wheels so that the vehicle will not roll into traffic if the brakes fail: headed downhill, turn the front wheels into the curb or toward the side of the road; where there is no curb, turn the wheels so the vehicle would roll away from the centre of the road. Always set the parking brake. Virginia's manual shows the same idea in diagrams. Learn the three cases from your own state's handbook, with its pictures: downhill, uphill with a curb, and no curb.

Other places named in Virginia's list

Virginia's manual also forbids parking in a bike lane, on the hard surface of a road where there is no curb, and within 500 feet of where fire trucks or equipment are stopped answering an alarm.

Test tip

The hydrant distance is the classic question: 15 feet in California and Virginia, 10 feet in Oregon. Check your own state.
