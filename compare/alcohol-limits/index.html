Alcohol limits (BAC): California, Virginia, Oregon

Every knowledge test asks about alcohol. The adult limit is the same number in these three states; the rules for young drivers differ.

The table shows what the three handbooks say, as we read them on 12 November 2026. "Not in the passages we read" means only that: the handbook may well cover the point elsewhere, so look it up there.

RuleCaliforniaVirginiaOregon
Adult drivers, 21 and overIllegal at 0.08% BAC or higherDriving under the influence at .08 percent or higherPresumed impaired at 0.08% or more
Drivers under 21Illegal at 0.01% or higherIllegal consumption at .02 or more and less than .08A zero tolerance law is mentioned; see the manual for its terms
Below the adult limitNot in the passages we readYou can be convicted with a lower BAC if your driving is impairedYou can still be arrested with a BAC lower than 0.08%
Open containersIllegal to drink alcohol, or to smoke or eat a cannabis product, while driving or riding as a passengerNot in the passages we readIllegal to have an open container in a vehicle on any road; a broken seal counts as open

0.08 is not a safe level

The handbooks are careful about this. Virginia's manual says a driver can be convicted with a BAC under .08 if driving is impaired, and Oregon's says you can be arrested below 0.08%. The figure is the point at which the law treats impairment as proven, not a line below which driving after drinking is acceptable. The only safe amount before driving is none.

Young drivers

For drivers under 21 the limits are close to zero: 0.01% in California and .02 in Virginia. A test question that asks for "the legal limit" will say which age group it means. Read it twice.

Other points in the handbooks

  • California's handbook gives 0.01% for a driver of any age on DUI probation, and lists a lower limit for commercial drivers.
  • California: refusing a chemical test leads to suspension or revocation of the driving privilege.
  • Oregon: marijuana impairment is treated as seriously as alcohol, and using both together reduces ability further.
  • Virginia: a person under 21 convicted with a BAC of at least .02 faces a one-year suspension and a fine or community service.

Test tip

Learn three numbers for your state: the adult limit, the under-21 limit, and the commercial limit if your handbook gives one. Penalties are asked less often than limits.
