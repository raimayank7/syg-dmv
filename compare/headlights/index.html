Headlight rules: California, Virginia, Oregon

Headlight questions are about three things: when lights must be on, when to switch from high beam to low beam, and what to use in rain or fog.

The table shows what the three handbooks say, as we read them on 12 November 2026. "Not in the passages we read" means only that: the handbook may well cover the point elsewhere, so look it up there.

Wipers on, lights on

Virginia's manual states the rule most simply: you must use your headlights whenever you use your windshield wipers because of bad weather. California's handbook tells drivers to turn on wipers, low-beam headlights and defroster in rain. The reason is as much to be seen as to see.

High beams

High beams are for open roads with no other vehicles near. California's handbook says to use them on open country roads or dark city streets and to make sure you can stop within the distance your headlights light up. All three handbooks require dimming for other drivers; Oregon and Virginia give distances, and they are not the same, so learn your own. Oregon adds that when you must dim, fog lights and other auxiliary lights must be off.

When another driver does not dim

California's handbook advises: do not look directly into the oncoming headlights, look toward the right edge of your lane, and do not retaliate by keeping your own high beams on.

Fog

The instinct in fog is to use more light. The handbooks say the opposite: low beams, because high-beam light reflects off the fog back into your eyes. Slow down, and if you cannot see, pull well off the road.

Test tip

Distances for dimming are the usual question. Oregon: 500 feet oncoming and 350 feet following. Virginia: 200 feet when following.
