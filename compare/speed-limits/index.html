Speed limits without a sign: California, Virginia, Oregon

Every state sets speed limits that apply even where no sign is posted. They are favourite test questions because the numbers differ from state to state.

The table shows what the three handbooks say, as we read them on 12 November 2026. "Not in the passages we read" means only that: the handbook may well cover the point elsewhere, so look it up there.

A speed limit is a maximum, not a target

All three handbooks make the same point in different words. California calls it the Basic Speed Law: you may never drive faster than is safe for current conditions. Oregon's manual defines a speed limit as the maximum speed considered safe for the area under ideal driving conditions, and refers to its own basic rule. Virginia's manual says a speed limit is the maximum legal speed under ideal conditions, and that by law you must drive slower where a school zone or work zone limit is in effect or where conditions require it. In rain, fog, heavy traffic or near children, the safe speed is below the number on the sign, and a driver can be stopped for driving too fast for conditions while under the limit.

How these appear in the test

Questions usually name a place and ask for the limit "unless otherwise posted". Learn your own state's list exactly. Do not carry a number from another state: 25 in a Virginia business area is 20 in Oregon. California adds special cases: 15 mph within 100 feet of a railroad crossing where you cannot see the tracks for 400 feet in both directions, and no more than 10 mph when passing a bus or streetcar stopped at a safety zone.

A way to remember

Write your state's limits on one card, slowest first, and say them aloud daily for a week. Then cover the numbers and test yourself on the places.
