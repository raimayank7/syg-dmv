How to study the driver handbook

The handbook is the source of every question on the test. People who fail have usually skimmed it or relied on practice questions alone.

Get the right book

Download the current handbook from the official website of your state's licensing agency. Check that it is for your state and that it is the latest edition; Oregon's, for example, carries the years 2026-2027 on its cover. It is free. Many states also offer it in other languages and as audio.

First reading: start to finish

Read the whole book once without trying to memorise, over two or three sittings. The aim is to know what is in it and where. Mark the pages with numbers: speeds, distances, ages, alcohol limits.

Second pass: the numbers

Make one card of numbers for your state. For example, from Virginia's manual: 25, 35 and 55 MPH for the unposted limits; 2, 3 and 4 seconds for following; 100 feet for signalling; 15 feet from a hydrant, 20 from an intersection, 50 from a railroad crossing; 500 feet of visibility for headlights; .08 and .02 for alcohol. Say the card aloud daily. Numbers that sit side by side on a card are much easier to keep apart than numbers scattered through a hundred pages.

Third pass: signs and markings

Learn signs by shape and colour first, then by picture. An eight-sided red sign is always stop; a downward-pointing triangle is yield; orange marks a work zone, as Virginia's manual puts it. Then learn pavement lines: what a solid line, a broken line and a double line allow.

Fourth pass: right-of-way

These questions are about situations, not numbers: who goes first at a four-way stop, at a roundabout, when a school bus stops, when an emergency vehicle approaches. Draw each situation on paper and say the rule aloud. All three handbooks we read say to yield to traffic already in a roundabout.

Use practice questions to find gaps

A practice test is a measuring tool. When you miss a question, do not just note the right option; open the handbook at that topic and read the paragraph. Otherwise you learn the question, not the rule, and the real test will word it differently.

The day before and the day itself

  • Read your numbers card and look through the sign pictures once.
  • Sleep. A tired mind misreads questions.
  • In the test, read every word. "Unless otherwise posted", "under 21" and "not" change the answer.
  • If the test lets you skip and return, skip what you are unsure of.
  • Bring the documents the official site lists. Requirements differ by state and are strict.
