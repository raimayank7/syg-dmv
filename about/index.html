About DMV Practice

DMV Practice helps people prepare for the written knowledge test for a driver license in the United States. It has 90 practice questions for California, Virginia and Oregon, each written by us from a rule in that state's public driver handbook, with the rule explained.

Independent, not official

This is not a government website. It is not connected with any Department of Motor Vehicles, Department of Transportation or licensing agency, and it uses no official logo. Our questions are not the official test questions and we have not copied any state's question bank or handbook text. Passing our practice does not guarantee passing the real test.

The handbooks we read

We read these on 12 November 2026:

  • California Driver's Handbook, California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV), the online edition on dmv.ca.gov
  • Virginia Driver's Manual, Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV), the PDF on dmv.virginia.gov (file dated April 2026)
  • Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services (DMV), Oregon Department of Transportation, the 2026-2027 edition (PDF on oregon.gov)

Why only three states

We write questions only from a handbook that we have actually opened and read. The official sites of several other states did not allow their handbooks to be downloaded when we tried, so those states are not covered yet. We would sooner cover three states honestly than fifty from memory. Rules differ between states, sometimes on the very numbers a test asks for, so do not use one state's questions to prepare for another.

Limits

A handbook is revised from time to time and the law can change between editions. We read parts of each handbook, not every page, and some rules are therefore missing. The page for each state says what was not captured. Always study the current handbook of your own state, which is free on its official website.
